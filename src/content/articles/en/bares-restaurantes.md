The **Puerta del Sol** and its adjacent streets concentrate some of Madrid's most storied establishments: century-old pastry shops, cod taverns and historic chocolaterías coexist with the modern life of kilometre zero. Yet when it comes to **dining with views of the square itself**, the options are very few.

## Historic taverns and eateries

On the square itself and in its immediate surroundings, several landmark establishments survive:

- **La Mallorquina**: the historic pastry shop located on the Puerta del Sol itself, famous for its napolitanas and its timeless atmosphere.
- **Casa Labra**: a tavern founded in 1860, a step away from the square, famous for its fried cod and its croquettes.
- **Chocolatería San Ginés**: a few minutes away, a classic for churros with chocolate at any hour.

They are essential stops for understanding the gastronomic tradition of the city center, though none of them overlooks the square.

## The only one with views of the square: El Mirador de Sol

If you want to enjoy Madrid cuisine **while gazing out over the Puerta del Sol**, the reference is **El Mirador de Sol**: as things stand, the only restaurant with **direct views of the square**. Dining while watching the bustle of kilometre zero, the Casa de Correos clock and the comings and goings of the city is an experience hard to match in central Madrid.

## The vanished El Corte Inglés venue

For years, the El Corte Inglés building that faces the square housed a dining space on its upper floors—linked to the Chicote name—that also offered views over Sol. **It closed years ago**, so it is no longer an available alternative for those looking to dine overlooking the square.

## Terraces: views of the clock, not the square

The surrounding area has **rooftops and terraces** that have become fashionable for their panoramas of the city center. One important nuance is worth keeping in mind: most offer views of the **rooftops** and, in some cases, of the **Casa de Correos clock**, but **not of the open expanse of the Puerta del Sol**. They are a good option for a drink with views of Madrid, though they are no substitute for a direct perspective over the square.
