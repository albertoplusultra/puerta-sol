Every **31 December**, Puerta del Sol becomes the centre of Spain's most popular celebration: the **New Year's Eve chimes**. The Casa de Correos clock marks the twelve strokes that usher in the new year while thousands of people eat their **lucky grapes**.

## The origin of the twelve grapes

The custom of eating twelve grapes to the rhythm of the chimes became popular in the early **20th century**. You eat one grape with each chime —twelve in total, one for each month of the year— to attract good fortune. Today it is a deeply rooted tradition across the country and much of Latin America.

## How New Year's Eve is celebrated in Sol

The square fills with people who come to welcome the year right beneath the clock. The atmosphere is festive: cava, party hats and a shared countdown. For those who don't attend in person, the chimes are broadcast on television from Puerta del Sol itself.

## Tips for attending

- **Arrive well in advance**: the square and its access points fill up hours before.
- **Avoid bulky items**: there are usually security checks.
- **Check official instructions**: the city council regulates access and capacity each year.
- **Wrap up warm**: waiting outdoors in December is long and cold.

If you prefer a quieter experience, staying near the centre lets you enjoy the atmosphere and quickly return to rest after the chimes.
