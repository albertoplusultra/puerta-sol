**Puerta del Sol** owes its name to one of the gates of the old wall that protected Madrid in the late 15th century. Facing east, it was decorated with the image of a sun, a symbol that eventually gave the place its name.

## From city gate to square

When Madrid grew beyond its walls, the gate disappeared, but the name remained. The space became a gathering point and one of the town's main crossroads. Over time, the open area gained importance as a commercial and social centre.

## The great 19th-century redevelopment

The square's present shape comes from the ambitious redevelopment carried out between **1857 and 1862**. That intervention regularised the layout, expanded the space and gave it the semicircular form we recognise today. The **Real Casa de Correos**, built in the 18th century, remained as the main building presiding over the square.

## A stage in Spain's history

Puerta del Sol has witnessed decisive moments. On **2 May 1808**, the uprising of the people of Madrid against Napoleon's troops had one of its focal points here. Since then, the square has accumulated symbolic value as a place of gathering, celebration and protest.

## A centre that never stops

Today Puerta del Sol combines that historic weight with the everyday life of a great capital: shops, passers-by, street performers and travellers who use it as a starting point to discover Madrid.
