The **Real Casa de Correos clock** is the most famous clock in Spain. Set in the tower of the building that presides over Puerta del Sol, its chimes mark the arrival of each new year before millions of viewers.

## The Real Casa de Correos

The building was constructed in the 18th century as the headquarters of the postal service. It is the oldest structure in the square and, since 1866, has housed in its tower the clock that would become a national symbol. Today it is the **seat of the Presidency of the Community of Madrid**.

## History of the clock

The current mechanism was the work of Spanish watchmaker **José Rodríguez Losada**, who donated it to the city. It was installed in **1866** and has run almost continuously ever since. Above the tower, a golden ball descends to announce the moments leading up to the chimes.

## The clock and the chimes

Every **31 December**, the twelve strokes of this clock set the rhythm of the **lucky grapes**, a tradition broadcast on television across Spain and much of the Spanish-speaking world. Knowing its history helps explain why Puerta del Sol is the stage for the Spanish New Year's Eve.
